The provided document directly addresses the query regarding the abominable snowman, its Tibetan origin, and the journalist involved in the mistranslation.

**Relevant Information:**
*   **Journalist:** Henry Newman.
*   **Tibetan Name:** *Metohkangmi* (or *metoh kangmi*).
*   **Actual Meaning:** Roughly "filthy snowman" or "disgusting," referring to "somebody dressed in rags."
*   **Mistranslation:** Newman translated *metoh* as "abominable" instead of "filthy" or "disgusting."
*   **Context:** This occurred after Newman interviewed returning members of Charles Howard-Bury's 1921 Mount Everest expedition.

**Supporting Quotation:**
> "Journalist Henry Newman, who interviewed the returning members of the expedition, mistranslated the Tibetan name—metoh kangmi, which roughly means 'filthy snowman'—for the suspected yeti as 'abominable snowman' in his reporting."

> "When I asked him what name was applied to these men, he said 'Metoh Kangmi.' Kangmi means 'snow-men' and the word metoh I translated as 'abominable.' ... Later, I was told by a Tibetan expert that I had not quite got the force of the word metoh. It did not mean abominable quite so much as 'filthy' and 'disgusting,' somebody dressed in rags."
