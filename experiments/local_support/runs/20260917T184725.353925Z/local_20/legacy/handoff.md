**Relevant Information Observed:**
The text explains that the term "abominable snowman" originated from a mistranslation by journalist Henry Newman. The local coolies referred to tracks as belonging to the "Metohkangmi," which roughly translates to "filthy snowman." Newman misinterpreted "metoh" as "abominable" rather than "filthy" or "disgusting." This error was later adopted by Charles Howard-Bury in his book about the 1921 Mount Everest expedition.

**Exact Supporting Quotation:**
> "Journalist Henry Newman, who interviewed the returning members of the expedition, mistranslated the Tibetan name—metoh kangmi, which roughly means 'filthy snowman'—for the suspected yeti as 'abominable snowman' in his reporting."

> "When I asked him what name was applied to these men, he said 'Metoh Kangmi.' Kangmi means 'snow-men' and the word metoh I translated as 'abominable.' ... Later, I was told by a Tibetan expert that I had not quite got the force of the word metoh. It did not mean abominable quite so much as 'filthy' and 'disgusting,' somebody dressed in rags."
