### Evidence Handoff

**Relevant Information Observed:**
The term "abominable snowman" originated from a mistranslation by journalist Henry Newman. The original Tibetan phrase was *metoh kangmi*. While Newman translated *metoh* as "abominable," he later admitted that a Tibetan expert corrected him, explaining that *metoh* more accurately meant "filthy," "disgusting," or referred to "somebody dressed in rags." Thus, the literal meaning is closer to "filthy snowman." This mistranslation was popularized after Newman reported on it and Charles Howard-Bury included the term in his 1922 book about the Mount Everest reconnaissance expedition.

**Exact Supporting Quotation:**
> "Journalist Henry Newman, who interviewed the returning members of the expedition, mistranslated the Tibetan name—metoh kangmi, which roughly means 'filthy snowman'—for the suspected yeti as 'abominable snowman' in his reporting."
>
> "Later, I was told by a Tibetan expert that I had not quite got the force of the word metoh. It did not mean abominable quite so much as 'filthy' and 'disgusting,' somebody dressed in rags." (Henry Newman, letter to *The Times*, 1937)
