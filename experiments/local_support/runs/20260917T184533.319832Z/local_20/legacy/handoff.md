**Relevant Information Observed:**
The term "abominable snowman" originated from a mistranslation by journalist Henry Newman in 1921. Newman interviewed members of Charles Howard-Bury’s Mount Everest expedition regarding tracks found in the snow. The local coolies referred to the creature as *Metohkangmi*. Newman translated this as "abominable snowman," but later admitted in a letter to *The Times* (London) in 1937 that he had misinterpreted the Tibetan word *metoh*. He was informed by a Tibetan expert that *metoh* meant "filthy" or "disgusting" (referring to someone dressed in rags), rather than "abominable." Despite the error, the term "abominable snowman" stuck and was subsequently used by Howard-Bury in his published book.
